# Osaka for Westerners / 大阪

Osaka (大阪, Ōsaka) is Japan's second city and the hub of the Kansai region: a merchant town that is louder, cheaper,
friendlier and more food-obsessed than Tokyo, within 15–75 minutes by train of Kyoto, Nara, Kobe and Himeji. This
skill is a briefing for visitors, business travellers and people relocating from Europe, North America or
Australia. Figures were checked in October 2026 and carry their date.

## Orientation
- **Scale.** Osaka City: **2.81 million** residents (estimate, 1 October 2026), 24 wards (区, ku); Osaka Prefecture
  8.75 million (1 September 2026). With Kyoto and Kobe (the Keihanshin area) it is Japan's second metropolis.
- **Story.** Naniwa (難波) was an imperial capital in the 7th century; Toyotomi Hideyoshi built Osaka Castle from 1583
  and the Tokugawa took it in the 1615 siege. Under the shoguns Osaka became the "kitchen of the nation" (天下の台所)
  where rice and goods were traded — hence its merchant humour and the greeting *mōkarimakka?* ("making money?").
  It hosted Expo '70, Japan's first World Expo, and **Expo 2025** (13 April–13 October 2025, 25.58 million visitors)
  on the island of Yumeshima. Voters rejected merging the city into an "Osaka Metropolis" in 2015 and 2020.
- **Districts**:
  - *Kita* キタ — **Umeda** 梅田 / Osaka Station 大阪駅: department stores, offices, the Umeda Sky Building and the
    new Grand Green Osaka park (Umekita, opened 2024); *Nakanoshima* 中之島 (museums, city hall, riverside offices).
  - *Minami* ミナミ — **Namba** 難波, **Dōtonbori** 道頓堀 (the canal, the Glico runner sign), **Shinsaibashi** 心斎橋
    shopping arcade, Amerikamura, Kuromon market 黒門市場: nightlife and food.
  - *Honmachi* 本町 / *Yodoyabashi* 淀屋橋 along Midōsuji 御堂筋: the business spine between Kita and Minami.
  - *Tennōji* 天王寺 / *Abeno* 阿倍野: Abeno Harukas (300 m), Shitennō-ji temple, the retro Shinsekai 新世界 quarter.
  - *Shin-Osaka* 新大阪 (Yodogawa-ku): the shinkansen station, 4 min north of Umeda; *Osaka Castle* 大阪城 and the
    Osaka Business Park; bay area: Universal Studios Japan (Konohana-ku 此花区), Kaiyūkan aquarium, Yumeshima 夢洲.
  - Suburbs: Senri 千里, Minoh 箕面, Suita 吹田 (Expo '70 park), Toyonaka 豊中 near Itami airport.
- **Climate.** Summers are hotter than Tokyo's (36–38 °C days happen); rainy season early June to mid-July;
  typhoons August–October (Typhoon Jebi flooded and closed Kansai airport in 2018); mild winters (2–10 °C).
- **Earthquakes.** The Great Hanshin earthquake (1995) killed over 6,000, mostly in Kobe. The government's
  September 2025 revision puts the 30-year chance of a **Nankai Trough** megaquake at 60 % to over 90 % (20–50 % under
  an alternative model); Osaka Bay faces tsunami risk — know your hotel's evacuation floor. Phones receive early
  warnings; the JNTO **Safety tips** app pushes alerts in English.

## Entering Japan and getting in from the airports
- **Visa waiver (verify at mofa.go.jp; hand off to `entry-requirements`).** 74 countries and regions are exempt (MOFA,
  September 2025). The **US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and EU/Schengen states** get up to **90 days**; **UK,
  Ireland, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Liechtenstein and Mexico** nationals can extend to six months at immigration
  in Japan (verify for the UK). Meetings and conferences are fine, **paid work is not**. Visa fees for those who need
  one rose to ¥15,000 single / ¥30,000 multiple from 1 July 2026.
- **JESTA**, Japan's planned ESTA-style pre-travel authorisation for visa-exempt visitors, is targeted for **fiscal
  2028** (April 2028–March 2029); date and fee not set (April 2026). Nothing to do yet — verify.
- **Visit Japan Web** (Digital Agency): register before flying and show one QR code at immigration and customs.
- **Departure tax** (International Tourist Tax): **¥3,000 since 1 July 2026** (was ¥1,000), included in the fare.
- **Kansai International (KIX)** 関西国際空港, on an island 50 km south-west of Umeda:
  - **JR Haruka** はるか limited express to Tennōji, Osaka Station (Umekita underground platforms since 2023,
    ~50 min), Shin-Osaka and **Kyoto** (~75–80 min; ¥3,590 reserved per May 2026 listings, and a cheaper one-way
    Haruka ticket for foreign passport holders, around ¥2,200 — verify at JR West).
  - **Nankai Rapi:t** ラピート to Namba 難波 in 34–40 min (about ¥1,490, 2026 guides — verify); the Nankai airport
    express takes ~45 min for under ¥1,000. Best for Minami hotels.
  - Limousine buses to Umeda, Namba, Kyoto, Kobe; the Kobe–KIX Bay Shuttle ferry (~30 min); a fixed-fare taxi or
    hotel shuttle for heavy luggage.
- **Itami (ITM)** 伊丹空港, Osaka International Airport: domestic flights only (Tokyo Haneda about 70 min). Osaka
  Monorail to Hotarugaike + Hankyu to Umeda (~25 min, about ¥440) or a limousine bus to Umeda (~30 min).
- **Kobe Airport (UKB)** 神戸空港 mainly domestic; began international charter flights in 2025 (verify routes).
- From Tokyo the shinkansen (Nozomi ~2 h 30 min Tokyo–Shin-Osaka, ¥14,720 reserved — verify) usually beats flying.

## Money
- Yen (¥). **No tipping**. Prices include 10 % consumption tax (8 % takeaway food).
- **Cash still matters**: cashless was 42.8 % of consumer spending nationally in 2024 (METI). Osaka's arcades,
  street-food stalls, small izakaya, Kyoto temples and Nara shrines often want cash. Carry ¥10,000–20,000.
- **ATMs for foreign cards**: Seven Bank in every 7-Eleven (24 h, English), Japan Post Bank, AEON, Lawson,
  FamilyMart; most big-bank machines refuse them. About ¥110–220 per withdrawal plus your bank's fee (2026).
- **Tax-free shopping changed on 1 November 2026** (verify with Japan Customs): pay the full tax-inclusive price
  (passport shown; minimum ¥5,000 before tax per shop per day); when leaving, do the customs check at a kiosk or via
  Visit Japan Web **before check-in** at KIX, then the shop refunds the tax. Goods must be bought within **90 days**
  of departure and carried out with you; every item on the receipt must be present. Shinsaibashi, Umeda department
  stores and Namba are the big tax-free zones. Hand off to `tax-refund-shopping`.

## Getting online and the apps that matter
- No firewall; a **travel eSIM** or airport pocket Wi-Fi is easiest; a voice SIM needs a residence card.
- **Apps**: Google Maps (excellent train routing), NAVITIME, **GO** (taxis, English, foreign cards; Uber also books
  taxis), **Tabelog** 食べログ (3.5+ is very good; Osaka rates harshly), **TableCheck** / **Omakase** (bookings),
  Google Translate camera for menus, **LINE** (messaging used by shops and landlords), **JR West** **WESTER** app
  and **e5489** (JR West tickets), Osaka Metro's app, JNTO **Safety tips**.

## Getting around
- **IC cards**: **ICOCA** (JR West) — Suica and PASMO from Tokyo work too — for all trains, buses, shops. Buy an ICOCA at
  KIX station; iPhone users can add a Suica to Apple Wallet (Welcome Suica Mobile
  works here as well).
- **Who runs what**: **Osaka Metro** (8 lines; the red Midōsuji line 御堂筋線 links Shin-Osaka, Umeda, Honmachi,
  Namba and Tennōji; the Chūō line now reaches Yumeshima), JR West (Osaka Loop Line 大阪環状線, to Kyoto, Kobe,
  Nara), and private lines that serve their own cities: **Hankyu** 阪急 (Umeda–Kyoto Kawaramachi, Kobe Sannomiya),
  **Keihan** 京阪 (Yodoyabashi–Kyoto Gion-Shijō), **Kintetsu** 近鉄 (Namba–Nara, Ise), **Hanshin** 阪神 (Umeda–Kobe),
  **Nankai** 南海 (Namba–KIX, Kōyasan). Same city, separate stations: Umeda has JR, Hankyu, Hanshin and three metro
  stations — allow 10 minutes to change.
- **Day trips by train** (approximate): Kyoto 30 min by JR Special Rapid from Osaka Station (15 min by shinkansen from
  Shin-Osaka), 45 min by cheaper Hankyu or Keihan to central Kyoto; Nara 35–40 min by Kintetsu from Namba; Kobe
  Sannomiya 20 min by JR; Himeji 30 min by shinkansen or ~60 min by JR Special Rapid.
- **Passes**: the **Japan Rail Pass** (¥50,000 for 7 days on the official site, ¥53,000 from agents since
  1 October 2026) rarely pays for a Kansai-based trip; regional JR West passes (Kansai Area, Kansai Wide) or the
  **Osaka Amazing Pass** (metro rides plus free entry to many sights) are usually better (verify prices). Hand off
  to `getting-around-asia`.
- **Escalators**: Osaka stands on the **right** (Tokyo and Kyoto stand on the left). Trains are quieter than the
  streets: no phone calls, backpack in front.
- **Taxis**: metered (Osaka tariff revised November 2025), plentiful in Kita and Minami; show the address in
  Japanese or use **GO**. Last trains around midnight.
- **Coming**: the Naniwasuji Line なにわ筋線, a direct Umeda–Namba link that cuts KIX–Umeda to about 30 min, is
  targeted for spring 2031.

## Business scene and customs
- **Why it matters.** Kansai is a manufacturing and pharma heartland: Panasonic (Kadoma), Daikin, Suntory, Kubota,
  Takeda, Shionogi and the Dōshōmachi 道修町 pharma district in Osaka; Sharp in Sakai; Nintendo, Kyocera, Murata,
  Omron and Shimadzu in Kyoto; Kobe Steel, Asics and Sysmex in Kobe. Biotech clusters around Osaka University (Suita)
  and the Kobe Biomedical Innovation Cluster on Port Island.
- **Customs** are the national ones: **meishi** 名刺 exchanged with both hands and kept on the table; seniority
  decides seating (kamiza 上座, farthest from the door) and speaking order; decisions by consensus (**nemawashi**
  根回し, **ringi** 稟議), so meetings confirm rather than decide; "that's difficult" (難しい) usually means no;
  punctuality is absolute; **nomikai** 飲み会 after work builds trust (pour for others, wait for *kanpai* 乾杯).
- **The Osaka difference**: people are more direct, joke more and talk money and price openly; haggling is
  acceptable in electronics shops and markets; relationships and face time matter even more than in Tokyo, and a
  good-humoured visitor who enjoys the food wins friends. Kyoto business is the opposite: indirect, slow,
  introduction-based.

## Living here
- **Where expats live**: Kita-ku (Umeda, Nakanoshima, Ōgimachi) and Nishi-ku 西区 (Utsubo Park 靭公園, Horie 堀江)
  for central flats; Fukushima 福島 for food; Tennōji; families choose **Minoh / Senri** (near Osaka International
  School) or commute from **Kobe** (Rokko Island 六甲アイランド near Canadian Academy, Higashinada) and the
  Ashiya 芦屋 / Nishinomiya 西宮 suburbs; academics often live in Kyoto.
- **Costs** (approximate, 2026): noticeably cheaper than Tokyo. Average listed rent for a single-person unit in the
  six central wards ¥89,173 (October 2025, LIFULL HOME'S); a 1LDK around Umeda roughly ¥90,000–140,000 (January 2026
  data); family flats ¥200,000–400,000. Move-in costs 4–6 months' rent, though "no key money, no guarantor" listings
  for foreigners are increasingly common. A lunch set ¥800–1,200.
- **Arriving to live**: residence card (在留カード) issued at KIX; within **14 days** register at the **ward office**
  (区役所), join National Health Insurance and the pension, apply for a My Number card; rubbish sorting by ward
  rules on set days with designated bags; few public bins.
- **Health**: ambulance **119**, police **110**; JNTO Visitor Hotline **050-3816-2787** (24 h, English). Larger
  hospitals with international or English support (verify current services): Osaka University Hospital
  大阪大学医学部附属病院 (Suita), Osaka Red Cross Hospital 大阪赤十字病院 (Tennōji), Sumitomo Hospital 住友病院
  (Nakanoshima), Yodogawa Christian Hospital 淀川キリスト教病院; in Kobe, Kobe Adventist Hospital. The AMDA
  International Medical Information Center gives referrals.
- **Schools** (verify fees and places): Osaka International School of Kwansei Gakuin (Minoh, IB), Osaka YMCA
  International School (Kita-ku), One World International School Osaka (opened 2023, IB/IGCSE); in Kobe, Canadian
  Academy (Rokko Island, IB), Marist Brothers International School, St. Michael's International School; Kyoto
  International School.

## Food
- **Kuidaore** 食い倒れ, "eat till you drop", is the Osaka motto. Specialities: **takoyaki** たこ焼き (octopus balls)
  and **okonomiyaki** お好み焼き (the *konamon* flour dishes), **kushikatsu** 串カツ in Shinsekai (one dip in the shared
  sauce only: 二度づけ禁止), kitsune udon, battera pressed sushi, Kuromon market seafood (touristy and pricey) and the
  2.6 km **Tenjinbashisuji** 天神橋筋 arcade, Japan's longest, for cheap local eating.
- Around Kansai: Kyoto kaiseki 懐石, yudōfu tofu and Uji 宇治 matcha; **Kobe beef** 神戸ビーフ (only from certified
  Tajima cattle; teppanyaki in Sannomiya; "Kobe beef" abroad is often not); Nara kakinoha-zushi 柿の葉寿司; Akashi-yaki.
- **Reservations**: high-end and kaiseki places book weeks ahead (TableCheck, Omakase, hotel concierge; no-shows are
  charged). Hand off to `restaurant-booking` / `michelin-dining`.
- **Dietary needs**: dashi (fish stock) is everywhere, so "vegetable" dishes rarely are; Kyoto's **shōjin ryōri**
  精進料理 temple food is the safest vegetarian option; halal and vegan choices are growing in Namba and Kyoto; carry
  a Japanese allergy card. Hand off to `dining-dietary-translate`.

## What to see (a weekend plus day trips)
- **Osaka, day 1**: Osaka Castle 大阪城 (park and museum), Nakanoshima, Umeda Sky Building's Floating Garden at
  sunset, evening in Dōtonbori and Hōzenji Yokochō 法善寺横丁.
- **Osaka, day 2**: Shitennō-ji 四天王寺 (founded 593), Abeno Harukas observatory, Shinsekai and Tsūtenkaku
  通天閣, Sumiyoshi Taisha 住吉大社, then Universal Studios Japan (Super Nintendo World; timed-entry tickets) or
  Kaiyūkan aquarium. Expo '70 Commemorative Park (Tower of the Sun) in Suita.
- **Expo 2025 legacy**: the Yumeshima site is being redeveloped; in September 2025 local and national governments
  agreed to keep a 200 m section of the wooden **Grand Ring** as the centrepiece of a new park, with plans for a
  wider leisure zone (verify what is open).
- **Osaka IR (casino resort)**: **MGM Osaka**, Japan's first integrated resort with a casino, broke ground on
  Yumeshima on 24 April 2025; investment about ¥1.27 trillion; opening targeted for **autumn 2030** (verify).
  Japanese residents will pay a ¥6,000 casino entry levy; foreign visitors do not.
- **Kyoto** 京都: Fushimi Inari 伏見稲荷 (go at dawn), Kiyomizu-dera 清水寺, Gion 祇園 (private alleys closed to
  tourists since 2024; never photograph geiko without consent), Kinkaku-ji 金閣寺, Arashiyama 嵐山 bamboo grove.
  Buses are overloaded: use the subway, trains and walking. Kyoto's accommodation tax rose on 1 March 2026 to
  ¥200–10,000 per person per night depending on the room rate (¥10,000 at ¥100,000+).
- **Nara** 奈良: Tōdai-ji 東大寺 and its Great Buddha, the deer park (bow, and keep food in your bag), Kasuga Taisha
  春日大社; half a day.
- **Kobe** 神戸: harbour and Meriken Park, the Kitano 北野 foreign residences, Mount Rokko night view, Arima Onsen
  有馬温泉 (one of Japan's oldest hot springs), Nada sake breweries.
- **Himeji Castle** 姫路城, the finest original castle (UNESCO): ¥2,500 for adults who are not Himeji residents since
  1 March 2026 (residents ¥1,000; under 18 free). Also: Kōyasan 高野山 temple stays, Ise Jingū.
