# Hanoi for Westerners / Hà Nội

Hanoi (Hà Nội, "the city inside the river") is Vietnam's capital: a thousand-year-old city of lakes, temples,
French villas and tube houses, with the Old Quarter's street life, the ministries and embassies, and a slower,
more formal and more traditional feel than Saigon. It has real seasons — a damp, chilly winter and a hot, stormy
summer — and serious winter air pollution. This skill is a briefing for visitors, business travellers and people
relocating from Europe, North America or Australia. Figures were checked in October 2026 and carry their date.

## Orientation
- **Scale.** About 8.7 million people on 3,360 km² (2025, Vietcetera); Vietnam's second city by population and
  economy after HCMC, and its political, diplomatic and university centre. Hà Tây province was absorbed in 2008.
- **Story.** Founded as **Thăng Long** ("ascending dragon") by Emperor Lý Thái Tổ in **1010**; the Temple of
  Literature dates from 1070. The Nguyễn dynasty moved the court to Huế in 1802 and the city was renamed Hà Nội in
  1831. Capital of French Indochina from 1902 (the Opera House, the French Quarter, Long Biên bridge). Hồ Chí Minh
  declared independence at Ba Đình on **2 September 1945**; the Việt Minh took the city in 1954; it was bombed in
  December 1972 and became capital of reunified Vietnam in 1976.
- **The 2025 reform.** Hanoi kept its borders when Vietnam cut its provinces from 63 to 34, but on **1 July 2025**
  the district level was abolished nationwide: Hanoi's 30 districts and 526 wards/communes became **126 units (51
  wards, 75 communes)**. **People, taxis and landlords still say "quận Hoàn Kiếm", "Tây Hồ", "Ba Đình"**; official
  addresses, contracts and residence paperwork use the new ward. Give both.
- **Neighbourhoods** (old district → new wards, Vietcetera, August 2025):
  - *Hoàn Kiếm* → Hoàn Kiếm and Cửa Nam wards: the lake, the **Old Quarter** (phố cổ, the "36 streets" named after
    guild trades), the French Quarter, Opera House, St Joseph's Cathedral, Hanoi railway station nearby.
  - *Ba Đình* → Ba Đình ward and neighbours: Hồ Chí Minh Mausoleum, government, embassies, Kim Mã, Lotte Center.
  - *Tây Hồ* → Tây Hồ ward (from Bưởi, Quảng An, Yên Phụ and others): West Lake (Hồ Tây), the expat heartland.
  - *Hai Bà Trưng, Đống Đa*: dense residential, universities, hospitals (Bạch Mai, Hanoi French Hospital).
  - *Cầu Giấy, Nam Từ Liêm (Mỹ Đình)*: new offices, Korean and Japanese communities, the National Convention Centre.
  - *Long Biên* across the Red River (Vingroup HQ, Vinhomes Riverside); *Hà Đông* south-west (end of metro 2A).
- **Climate.** Four seasons. **Winter December–February: 10–20 °C, damp and grey, no central heating** (air-con
  units double as heaters; bring layers). **Nồm** (February–April) makes walls and floors sweat. Summer May–September
  is 30–38 °C with heavy rain and typhoons (Yagi flooded the north in September 2024). Best: October–November and
  March–April. **Air pollution** is the main health issue: worst from November to February when inversions trap
  smoke and traffic fumes; the city ranked among the world's five most polluted on several days in winter 2025–26
  (AQI 225 on 30 November 2025, IQAir) and averaged PM2.5 45 µg/m³ in 2024, about nine times the WHO guideline.
  Check IQAir/AirVisual daily, use N95 masks and run purifiers at home. Don't drink tap water.

## Entering Vietnam and getting in from the airport
- **Visa exemption (checked October 2026; verify at the Immigration Department).** 45 days visa-free for ordinary
  passports of **Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the UK, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland**, Russia, Japan and South
  Korea (Resolution 44/NQ-CP, 15 March 2025 – 14 March 2028) and, since 15 August 2025, **Belgium, Bulgaria,
  Croatia, Czech Republic, Hungary, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia and Switzerland**
  (Resolution 229/NQ-CP, to 14 August 2028). **The US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, Austria, Portugal
  and Greece are not exempt** and need an e-visa.
- **E-visa**: all nationalities, up to **90 days, single (US$25) or multiple entry (US$50)**, non-refundable; apply
  1–2 weeks ahead at **evisa.gov.vn** (Immigration Department, Ministry of Public Security; also
  evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn). **Lookalike sites** (.org/.com "vietnam-evisa…") charge several times the fee — only
  .gov.vn is official. Print the approval and check every detail; passport valid 6 months.
- **No arrival card**: immigration only checks passport and visa. Hotels register guests with the police (within 12
  hours); a host in a private home must do it.
- **Nội Bài (HAN)**, about 30 km north (45–60 min): T2 international, T1 domestic. To the centre: Grab / Xanh SM /
  Be from the app pickup point, or metered Mai Linh / G7 / Taxi Group from the official rank (guides quote
  ₫300,000–450,000 to the Old Quarter, 2026; agree "by meter"); **Express bus 86** from outside T2 (and T1) to the
  Old Quarter edge and Hanoi railway station, ₫45,000 (2026 guides; pay the conductor). Ignore touts in the hall.

## Money
- Currency is the đồng (VND, ₫); roughly ₫26,000–27,000 to the US$ in 2026 (check market_data). Notes ₫1,000 to
  ₫500,000; the polymer ₫10,000/₫200,000 and ₫20,000/₫500,000 look alike — count carefully. "50k" = ₫50,000.
- **Cash** for street food, markets, xe ôm and small cafés. Locals pay by **VietQR** bank-transfer QR codes from a
  Vietnamese banking app; visitors without a local account usually cannot (NAPAS VietQRGlobal works for some Asian
  wallets; Mastercard's scan-to-pay was rolling out in August 2026). Residents with a temporary residence card can
  open a bank account.
- Visa and Mastercard work in hotels, malls, supermarkets, chains and better restaurants; ATMs of Vietcombank,
  Techcombank, VPBank or HSBC are reliable; most charge a per-withdrawal fee; refuse dynamic currency conversion.
- **Tipping** is not expected; tip guides and drivers on tours and good spa staff. Upscale places add 5 % service.
  Haggle in markets and with cyclos (agree the price and the duration first).

## Getting online and the apps that matter
- Google, WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram work; 4G/5G is fast and cheap.
- **SIM / eSIM**: every SIM is registered to a passport with a face photo, and new subscriptions since 15 April 2026
  need facial biometric checks (secondary report, verify). Easiest: a travel eSIM bought before the flight, or a
  Viettel / Vinaphone / Mobifone tourist SIM at the Nội Bài counters.
- **Must-have apps**: **Grab** (cars, bikes, food, card payment, English), **Xanh SM** (Vingroup's all-electric
  VinFast taxis and bikes, born in Hanoi in 2023 and now everywhere), **Be**, **Zalo** (the national messenger used
  by landlords, offices and drivers), **Google Maps** (works well), ShopeeFood / GrabFood, **IQAir** for the air.

## Getting around
- **Crossing the street**: walk slowly and steadily, never run or step back; drivers steer around a predictable
  pedestrian. Old Quarter pavements are parking lots and kitchens — you will walk in the road.
- **Riding a motorbike** legally needs a Vietnamese licence, or a **1968-Convention** International Driving Permit
  with a motorcycle category plus your home licence; the 1949 IDP issued in the US, Canada and Australia is
  generally not accepted (verify). Unlicensed riders lose their insurance cover — relevant for the Hà Giang loop.
  **Fines rose sharply on 1 January 2025** (Decree 168/2024): red light ₫4–6 million on a motorbike, no helmet
  ₫400,000–600,000, any alcohol (zero tolerance) ₫2 million up to ₫8–10 million plus licence points; cameras
  fine remotely.
- **Low-emission zone**: Hanoi plans to push petrol motorbikes out of the centre. A pilot from July 2026 limits
  petrol motorbikes on 11 Old Quarter / lake streets on weekend evenings; the full Ring Road 1 zone was pushed back
  towards 2028 (reports April–June 2026; check what is enforced).
- **Metro** (Hanoi Metro, 05:30–22:00, checked October 2026): **Line 2A Cát Linh – Hà Đông** (elevated, 13 km, 12
  stations, opened 6 November 2021) and **Line 3 Nhổn – Cầu Giấy** (elevated section opened August 2024; the
  underground extension to Hanoi railway station is planned for late 2027). Fares follow Decision 3316/QĐ-UBND
  (June 2025); older published fares were ₫8,000–15,000 a trip plus day and monthly passes (verify); tickets at
  station counters and the Hà Nội Metro app. About 20 million trips in 2025. Useful mainly for Đống Đa, Cầu Giấy
  and Hà Đông — the Old Quarter and Tây Hồ are not yet on the metro.
- **Taxis**: apps, Xanh SM, or Mai Linh / G7 / Taxi Group; avoid unmarked cars and anyone refusing the meter.
  Buses are cheap; e-buses (VinBus) run several routes.
- **Intercity**: HCMC is about 2 h by air (dozens of flights a day); Đà Nẵng 1 h 20. The **Reunification Express**
  leaves Hanoi station (ga Hà Nội, Lê Duẩn) for Huế, Đà Nẵng and Saigon (30+ hours end to end; dsvn.vn); night trains
  to Lào Cai for Sa Pa; limousine vans to Hạ Long, Ninh Bình and Sa Pa.

## Business scene and customs
- **Why it matters.** The seat of the Communist Party, National Assembly, ministries and embassies — where licences,
  approvals and state-owned partners are. Big local groups: Viettel, Vingroup, FPT, the state banks. The northern
  manufacturing belt (Samsung, Canon, Foxconn and suppliers in Bắc Ninh, Thái Nguyên and Hải Phòng) is 1–2 h away.
- **Customs**: more formal and hierarchical than Saigon. Exchange cards with both hands; greet the most senior and
  oldest first; titles matter (anh/chị/ông/bà); expect tea and small talk, then several meetings; criticism in
  public loses face; "we will study it" often means no; decisions come from the top and from relationships with
  officials. Dinners seal trust; toasts ("một, hai, ba, zô!" or "trăm phần trăm" — bottoms up) are frequent, but
  you can stop if you say so early. Zalo is the business channel. Lunch 11:30–13:30 with a nap.
- **Tết** closes the country for about a week and slows it for two to three: Tết 2027 falls on **6 February 2027**.
  Hanoi empties as migrants go home. Other holidays: 30 April – 1 May, 2 September (National Day, parades at Ba Đình).
- **Working legally**: a work permit (up to 2 years, renewable once, under Decree 219/2025 effective 7 August 2025;
  usually a degree plus relevant experience), then a **temporary residence card (TRC, thẻ tạm trú)** tied to it;
  employers file both. Working on an e-visa or exemption is illegal; avoid "visa agents" offering shortcuts.

## Living here
- **Where expats live**: **Tây Hồ** (West Lake: Quảng An, Xuân Diệu, Tô Ngọc Vân, Đặng Thai Mai) — villas, serviced
  apartments, Western cafés and bakeries, lakeside cycling; the expat village. **Ba Đình** (Kim Mã, Liễu Giai,
  Văn Phúc diplomatic compound) for embassies and offices. **Ciputra** (north-west of the lake) for gated houses
  near UNIS. **Hoàn Kiếm / French Quarter** for central living. **Cầu Giấy / Mỹ Đình** and **Vinhomes** towers
  (Times City, Ocean Park, Smart City) for newer, cheaper flats.
- **Costs**: 2026 Hanoi rent figures are not in this data; typical serviced one-bedrooms in Tây Hồ are a little
  below HCMC's Thảo Điền equivalents — get current listings. Landlords want one to two months' deposit and must
  register you for residence. Budget for air purifiers and dehumidifiers.
- **Health**: **Hanoi French Hospital** (Bệnh viện Việt Pháp, 1 Phương Mai, Đống Đa), **Vinmec Times City** (458
  Minh Khai, Hai Bà Trưng; JCI-accredited group), **Family Medical Practice Hanoi** (Kim Mã area, Ba Đình),
  **Raffles Medical Hanoi** (51 Xuân Diệu, Tây Hồ), Hồng Ngọc (private); Việt Đức (trauma) and Bạch Mai are the
  big public hospitals (UK FCDO list, 2022; confirm addresses). Ambulance 115, police 113, fire 114 — call the
  private hospital's emergency line first. Complex cases are often evacuated to Bangkok or Singapore: carry
  insurance with evacuation cover. Dengue peaks in late summer and autumn.
- **Schools**: United Nations International School (UNIS Hanoi, Ciputra), British International School Hanoi (BIS),
  Concordia International School, St. Paul American School, British Vietnamese International School (BVIS Hanoi),
  Lycée Français Alexandre Yersin, Hanoi International School. Fees for 2025–26 were indicatively ₫500–950 million a
  year depending on grade (Tutopiya guide); confirm with each school and apply early.
- **Daily life and safety**: very safe; the risks are traffic, pickpocketing in the Old Quarter, bag snatching,
  pollution and the cold. Scams: rigged taxi meters, cyclo price "misunderstandings", shoe-shine and fruit-seller
  photo charges, overpriced Hạ Long tours sold on the street — book with reviewed operators. **Train Street** (phố
  đường tàu) cafés are periodically closed by police; don't stand on the tracks. Drugs carry severe penalties.
  Supermarkets: WinMart, Lotte Mart, AEON (Long Biên, Hà Đông), Annam Gourmet and Tây Hồ delis for imports.

## Food
- **Hanoi classics**: **phở bò** (Hanoi-style: clear beef broth, few herbs, no sauces — Phở Thìn Lò Đúc, Phở Bát Đàn
  queue), **bún chả** (grilled pork with rice noodles; Hương Liên became the "Obama bún chả" after 2016), **chả cá
  Lã Vọng** (turmeric fish with dill), bún thang, bánh cuốn, bún đậu mắm tôm, xôi (sticky rice), nem rán. **Egg
  coffee** (cà phê trứng, Café Giảng since 1946), coconut coffee at Cộng, and **bia hơi** — fresh draught beer on
  plastic stools (Tạ Hiện "beer street").
- **Street-food hygiene**: eat where it is busy and cooked to order; wash-up water at stalls is the main risk; ice
  is factory-made. Vegetarian: chay restaurants ("tôi ăn chay" = I am vegetarian).
- **Michelin Guide Vietnam** 2026 edition (4 June 2026, ceremony in Tây Hồ): four one-star restaurants in Hanoi —
  Gia, Hibana by Koki, Tầm Vị, and ONVIT (new; the first Korean star in Vietnam) — plus many Bib Gourmand
  street-food places (guide.michelin.com).

## What to see (a weekend plus day trips)
- **Centre**: Hoàn Kiếm Lake and Ngọc Sơn Temple (pedestrian streets around the lake Friday–Sunday evenings), the
  Old Quarter and its night market, **Temple of Literature** (Văn Miếu – Quốc Tử Giám), **Hồ Chí Minh Mausoleum**
  (mornings only, closed some weekdays and for annual maintenance — check), the Presidential Palace grounds and
  One Pillar Pagoda, **Imperial Citadel of Thăng Long** (UNESCO), **Hỏa Lò Prison** ("Hanoi Hilton"), **Vietnam
  Museum of Ethnology** (Cầu Giấy, the best museum in the country), Vietnamese Women's Museum, the Opera House,
  Trấn Quốc Pagoda on West Lake, Long Biên bridge at sunset, a water-puppet show (Thăng Long theatre).
- **Day trips and overnights**: **Hạ Long Bay** (Quảng Ninh, about 2.5 h by expressway; take an overnight cruise;
  quieter Lan Hạ Bay via Cát Bà / Hải Phòng — the UNESCO site was extended to Cát Bà in 2023); **Ninh Bình**
  (about 2 h; Tràng An boat trip, Tam Cốc, the old capital Hoa Lư; UNESCO 2014); **Sa Pa** (Lào Cai, 5–6 h by
  expressway bus or a night train to Lào Cai; rice terraces, trekking with Hmong and Dao guides, Fansipan cable
  car); Bát Tràng ceramics village, the Perfume Pagoda (Chùa Hương, spring pilgrimage), Mai Châu, and the Hà Giang
  loop (go with a licensed rider-guide).
