# Ho Chi Minh City for Westerners / Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh

Ho Chi Minh City (Thành phố Hồ Chí Minh, TP.HCM; still called **Saigon / Sài Gòn** by nearly everyone) is
Vietnam's commercial capital and largest city: millions of motorbikes, French colonial boulevards, glass towers,
street food on every pavement and a young, entrepreneurial population. It is cheap, friendly and easy for a
visitor, but loud, hot and chaotic; English is better than in Hanoi yet limited outside District 1 and Thảo Điền.
This skill is a briefing for visitors, business travellers and people relocating from Europe, North America or
Australia. Figures were checked in October 2026 and carry their date.

## Orientation
- **Scale.** Before July 2025 the city had about 9.9 million residents on 2,100 km². On **1 July 2025** it absorbed
  the provinces of **Bình Dương** (factories, north) and **Bà Rịa–Vũng Tàu** (oil, port, beaches, south-east):
  population now over 14 million, area roughly tripled, the most populous unit in Vietnam (Vietcetera, July 2025;
  no official post-merger census yet). It is Vietnam's largest economy, ahead of Hanoi.
- **Story.** A Khmer trading post (Prey Nokor) settled by Vietnamese (Gia Định, 1698); French from 1859 and capital
  of Cochinchina (the cathedral, post office, opera house); capital of South Vietnam 1955–1975; fell on **30 April
  1975** and renamed in 1976. Since the 1986 Đổi Mới reforms it has been the engine of Vietnam's growth.
- **The 2025 reform (what changed on paper).** Vietnam cut its provinces from 63 to 34 and **abolished the district
  level everywhere** on 1 July 2025; local government is now city → ward (phường) / commune (xã). HCMC has 168
  ward/commune units (102 inside the old city). Thủ Đức City (created 2021 from Districts 2, 9 and Thủ Đức) is gone
  too. **In daily life people, taxis, hotels, landlords and Grab still say "Quận 1", "Quận 7", "District 2"**;
  official addresses, contracts and residence paperwork use the new ward. Give both, e.g. "Thảo Điền (old District 2),
  An Khánh ward".
- **Neighbourhoods** (old name → new ward, Vietcetera mapping July 2025):
  - *District 1* Quận 1 → Sài Gòn, Bến Thành, Tân Định, Cầu Ông Lãnh wards: downtown, Nguyễn Huệ walking street,
    Đồng Khởi, Bến Thành market, Phạm Ngũ Lão / Bùi Viện backpacker street, Đa Kao.
  - *District 3* Quận 3 → Bàn Cờ, Xuân Hòa, Nhiêu Lộc: leafy villas, cafés, consulates.
  - *District 2 / Thảo Điền & Thủ Thiêm* → An Khánh ward: the expat village across the river; Thủ Thiêm is the new CBD.
  - *District 5 / Chợ Lớn* → Chợ Lớn, An Đông, Chợ Quán: Chinatown, Bình Tây market, temples.
  - *District 7 / Phú Mỹ Hưng* → Tân Mỹ, Tân Hưng, Tân Thuận, Phú Thuận: planned Korean/Taiwanese/Japanese suburb,
    FV Hospital, SSIS.
  - *Bình Thạnh* → Bình Thạnh, Gia Định, Thạnh Mỹ Tây (Landmark 81, Vinhomes Central Park) and others.
  - *Tân Bình / Phú Nhuận*: the airport side. Former Bình Dương and Vũng Tàu now count as HCMC.
- **Climate.** Tropical, 25–35 °C all year. Dry season December–April (best for visits; March–April is hottest);
  rainy season May–November with short, violent afternoon downpours. Streets flood after heavy rain and at the
  **king tides of October–December**, worst in low-lying Districts 7, 8 and Bình Thạnh. Don't drink tap water.

## Entering Vietnam and getting in from the airports
- **Visa exemption (checked October 2026; verify at the Immigration Department).** 45 days visa-free for ordinary
  passports of **Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the UK, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland**, Russia, Japan and South
  Korea (Resolution 44/NQ-CP, valid 15 March 2025 – 14 March 2028) and, since 15 August 2025, **Belgium, Bulgaria,
  Croatia, Czech Republic, Hungary, Luxembourg, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia and Switzerland**
  (Resolution 229/NQ-CP, to 14 August 2028). ASEAN neighbours have 14–30 days. **The US, Canada, Australia, New
  Zealand, Ireland, Austria, Portugal and Greece are not exempt** and need an e-visa.
- **E-visa**: open to all nationalities, up to **90 days, single (US$25) or multiple entry (US$50)**, fee
  non-refundable; apply 1–2 weeks ahead on the official portal **evisa.gov.vn** (Immigration Department, Ministry of
  Public Security; also evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn). **Lookalike sites** (.org/.com "vietnam-evisa…") charge several
  times the fee — only .gov.vn is official. Print the approval and check every detail; passport valid 6 months.
- **No arrival card**: immigration only checks passport and visa. Hotels register guests with the police (within 12
  hours); a host in a private home must do it.
- **Tân Sơn Nhất (SGN)**, 7 km north of District 1: T2 international, T1 and the new **T3 domestic** (opened April
  2025). To downtown: Grab / Be / Xanh SM car from the app pickup zone, 20–45 min depending on traffic; **bus 109** to Bến Thành / Phạm Ngũ Lão, ₫15,000–20,000 cash (2026; it now
  starts at T3 — free airport shuttle from T1/T2). Ignore touts in the arrivals hall.
- **Long Thành (LTH)**, the new mega-airport 40 km east in Đồng Nai: technical inauguration 19 December 2025;
  **commercial opening proposed for 1 December 2026** (CAAV/ACV, reported July 2026; not yet confirmed and slipped
  before). Plan: international flights encouraged to move in winter 2026–27, all long-haul international from
  summer 2027; SGN keeps domestic. Allow 1–1.5 h to downtown by road. Always check which airport a ticket uses.

## Money
- Currency is the đồng (VND, ₫); roughly ₫26,000–27,000 to the US$ in 2026 (check market_data). Notes ₫1,000 to
  ₫500,000; the polymer ₫10,000 and ₫200,000, and ₫20,000 and ₫500,000, look alike — count carefully. Prices are
  often written "50k" = ₫50,000. US dollars are not legal tender for shops.
- **Cash is still king** at street stalls, markets, small cafés and xe ôm. Locals pay everything by **VietQR**
  (scan a bank-transfer QR code with a Vietnamese banking app); without a local account a visitor usually cannot.
  NAPAS's VietQRGlobal now lets some Asian wallets (Alipay, WeChat Pay, Korean and Thai apps) scan these codes, and
  Mastercard is rolling out scan-to-pay (August 2026) — not yet reliable for Western cards.
- Visa and Mastercard work in hotels, malls, supermarkets, chain cafés and mid-range restaurants; Apple/Google Pay
  increasingly. ATMs are everywhere; most charge a fee per withdrawal and cap at a few million đồng — choose bank
  ATMs (Vietcombank, Techcombank, VPBank, HSBC) and decline "dynamic currency conversion". Grab can be paid by card.
- Residents with a temporary residence card can open a local bank account and use VietQR.
- **Tipping** is not expected; rounding up, ₫20,000–50,000 for good spa/hotel service and a tip for guides and
  drivers on tours is appreciated. Upscale restaurants add 5 % service plus VAT. Haggle at markets, not in shops.

## Getting online and the apps that matter
- Google, WhatsApp, Facebook and Instagram all work; 4G/5G is fast and cheap.
- **SIM / eSIM**: every SIM is registered to a passport with a face photo; since 15 April 2026 new subscriptions
  need facial biometric verification (secondary report, verify). Easiest: a travel eSIM bought before the flight,
  or a tourist SIM from Viettel, Vinaphone or Mobifone at the airport counters (passport, a few minutes, cheap data).
- **Must-have apps**: **Grab** (cars, motorbikes, food, card payment, English), **Be** (Vietnamese rival, often
  cheaper), **Xanh SM** (Vingroup's all-electric VinFast taxis and bikes; app and street hail, fixed meter),
  **Zalo** (the national messenger — landlords, offices, clinics and drivers use it; WhatsApp is for foreigners),
  **Google Maps** (works well), ShopeeFood / GrabFood (delivery), Shopee (shopping), Google Translate for menus.

## Getting around
- **Motorbikes** rule the road. **Crossing the street**: walk slowly and steadily, never run or step back; drivers
  steer around a predictable pedestrian. Bikes ride on pavements and against traffic. Keep your phone away from the
  kerb — bag and phone snatching by passing bikes is the most common crime against visitors.
- **Riding yourself**: legally you need a Vietnamese licence or an International Driving Permit under the **1968
  Vienna Convention** with a motorcycle category plus your home licence; the 1949 IDP issued in the US, Canada and
  Australia is generally not accepted (verify). Unlicensed riding voids most travel insurance. **Fines rose
  sharply on 1 January 2025** (Decree 168/2024): red light ₫4–6 million on a motorbike, no helmet ₫400,000–600,000,
  any alcohol (zero tolerance) ₫2 million up to ₫8–10 million plus licence points; cameras fine remotely.
- **Metro Line 1** (Bến Thành – Suối Tiên), opened **22 December 2024**: 19.7 km, 14 stations (3 underground: Bến
  Thành, Opera House / Nhà hát Thành phố, Ba Son), stops at Thảo Điền and An Phú, end to end about 30 min;
  ₫6,000–20,000 a trip (2025–26), day and monthly passes, cashless ticket machines (check accepted cards); roughly
  5:00–22:00 (verify). Over 20 million trips in 2025. **Line 2** (Bến Thành – Tham Lương) began construction 15
  January 2026; no other line runs.
- **Taxis**: use the apps, Xanh SM, or the established metered brands **Vinasun** (white) and **Mai Linh** (green);
  avoid lookalikes and anyone who won't use the meter. Buses are cheap but slow.
- **Intercity**: Hanoi is about 2 h by air (Vietnam Airlines, Vietjet, Bamboo; dozens of flights a day). The **Reunification Express** (Thống Nhất) from Saigon station (ga Sài Gòn)
  to Hanoi takes 30+ hours; useful legs are to Nha Trang, Phan Thiết and Đà Nẵng (dsvn.vn). Limousine vans to
  Vũng Tàu, Đà Lạt and the Mekong.

## Business scene and customs
- **Why it matters.** Vietnam's private-sector and start-up capital: banks, consumer brands, logistics, real estate,
  FDI manufacturing in the industrial parks of former Bình Dương and Đồng Nai, Saigon Hi-Tech Park (Intel, Samsung
  electronics) in Thủ Đức, the new Thủ Thiêm financial district. More commercial and quicker than Hanoi, where
  ministries and licences sit.
- **Customs**: relationships first — expect several meetings, meals and coffee before a deal; give and receive
  business cards with both hands; greet the most senior person first, age and title matter; a smile and "we will
  consider" can mean no; nobody should lose face in public, so raise problems privately; decisions go up the
  hierarchy; dinners and beer (toast "một, hai, ba, dzô!") seal relationships, drinking is optional if you say so
  early. Zalo is the business channel; reply fast. Lunch 11:30–13:30, many offices nap.
- **Tết** (Lunar New Year) shuts the country for about a week and slows business for two to three: Tết 2027 falls on
  **6 February 2027**. Workers expect a 13th-month bonus before Tết. Other holidays: 30 April – 1 May, 2 September
  (National Day), Hùng Kings' day (spring).
- **Working legally**: a work permit (up to 2 years, renewable once, under Decree 219/2025 effective 7 August 2025;
  usually a degree plus relevant experience) and then a **temporary residence card (TRC, thẻ tạm trú)** tied to it;
  employers file both. Working on an e-visa or visa exemption is illegal; avoid "visa agents" offering shortcuts.

## Living here
- **Where expats live**: **Thảo Điền / An Phú** (old District 2): villas, serviced apartments, international
  schools, Western food, riverside bars — easy, green, a bubble, 15–30 min to District 1 (the metro helps).
  **District 1 and 3**: central, walkable by Saigon standards, noisy. **Phú Mỹ Hưng** (old District 7): planned,
  clean, quiet, families, Korean and Japanese communities, near FV Hospital and SSIS. **Bình Thạnh** (Vinhomes
  Central Park, Landmark 81): new high-rises between the two.
- **Costs** (2026, indicative): a furnished one-bedroom in a Thảo Điền tower ₫17–30 million a month on expat
  listings; ₫10–18 million for local-market contracts (aggregator, January 2026). Air-conditioning makes
  electricity the big bill. Landlords want one to two months' deposit and must register you for residence.
- **Health** (Australian DFAT list, July 2026): **FV Hospital** (Franco-Vietnamese, JCI-accredited, Tân Mỹ ward, old
  District 7), **Vinmec Central Park** (Thạnh Mỹ Tây ward, Bình Thạnh), **Family Medical Practice** (Diamond Plaza,
  District 1; 24/7 centre at 95 Thảo Điền; District 7), **Raffles Medical** (285B Điện Biên Phủ, District 3),
  Victoria Healthcare, American International Hospital (AIH). Ambulance 115, police 113, fire 114 — but call the
  private hospital's emergency line first; insurance with direct billing helps. Dengue peaks in the rainy season.
- **Schools**: British International School (BIS), International School HCMC (ISHCMC), Saigon South International
  School (SSIS, Phú Mỹ Hưng), Australian International School (AIS), British Vietnamese International School
  (BVIS), Lycée Français International Marguerite Duras. Fees rose 3–6 % for 2026–27; top grades exceed US$38,000 a
  year plus registration and deposits (Tuổi Trẻ, August 2026). Apply early.
- **Daily life and safety**: violent crime against foreigners is rare; the hazards are traffic, snatch theft, heat
  and flooding. Scams: taxis without meters, cyclo drivers who renegotiate, shoe-shiners and coconut sellers who
  demand payment for a photo, "friendly" card games, inflated bills in Bùi Viện bars — agree prices first. Drugs
  carry severe penalties, including death for trafficking. Supermarkets: Co.opmart, Lotte Mart, AEON, Annam Gourmet.

## Food
- **Saigon specialities**: **cơm tấm** (broken rice with grilled pork chop, the breakfast of the south), **bánh mì**
  (Huỳnh Hoa on Lê Thị Riêng is the famous queue), **hủ tiếu** (Chinese-Khmer noodle soup), southern-style **phở**
  (sweeter, with herbs and bean sprouts, unlike Hanoi's), bánh xèo, gỏi cuốn (fresh rolls), ốc (snail bars of
  District 4). **Coffee**: cà phê sữa đá (iced, with condensed milk), cà phê muối (salted), coconut coffee;
  chains (Highlands, Phúc Long, Cộng) and thousands of independent cafés.
- **Street-food hygiene**: eat where it is busy and cooked to order, avoid raw greens if your stomach is new, ice in
  cities is factory-made and generally fine. Vegetarian: chay restaurants (Buddhist, often near pagodas); "no meat"
  is "không thịt", "I am vegetarian" is "tôi ăn chay".
- **Michelin Guide Vietnam** 2026 edition (4 June 2026): six one-star restaurants in HCMC — Anan Saigon, Akuna,
  CieL, Coco Dining, Long Triều, Upstairs (new) — plus many Bib Gourmand street-food places (guide.michelin.com).

## What to see (a weekend plus day trips)
- **Downtown on foot**: Notre-Dame Cathedral (Nhà thờ Đức Bà; long restoration, check access) and the Central Post
  Office (Bưu điện Thành phố), Independence Palace (Dinh Độc Lập), the **War Remnants Museum** (Bảo tàng Chứng tích
  Chiến tranh; hard-hitting), Opera House, Nguyễn Huệ walking street, the Book Street, Bến Thành market (touristy;
  bargain), Landmark 81, the Jade Emperor Pagoda (Chùa Ngọc Hoàng); **Chợ Lớn** (Bình Tây market,
  Thiên Hậu temple); evenings on rooftop bars, Bùi Viện or a river dinner cruise.
- **Day trips**: **Củ Chi tunnels** (địa đạo Củ Chi, 60–70 km north-west; Bến Dược is less crowded than Bến Đình;
  go early), **Cao Đài Holy See** in Tây Ninh (combine with Củ Chi), the **Mekong Delta** (Mỹ Tho and Bến Tre for a
  day, Cần Thơ's floating market overnight — provinces were renamed in the 2025 merger), **Cần Giờ** mangroves,
  **Vũng Tàu** beaches and seafood (2 h by road, or by boat from Bạch Đằng; now part of HCMC), Long Hải. Further:
  Đà Lạt (highlands, 1 h flight), Phú Quốc (island, 1 h flight, 30-day visa-free for direct arrivals), Côn Đảo.
