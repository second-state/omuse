# Shanghai for Westerners / 上海指南

Shanghai (上海, Shànghǎi) is China's largest city and biggest urban economy: the financial centre, the main base of
multinationals in China, the busiest container port in the world and the most cosmopolitan city on the mainland,
with a treaty-port past still visible on the Bund and in the plane-tree streets of the former French Concession.
English goes further here than anywhere else in mainland China, but it is still not London. This skill is a briefing
for visitors, business travellers and people relocating from Europe, North America or Australia. Figures were checked
in October 2026 and carry their date.

## Orientation
- **Scale.** 24.85 million residents at end-2025 (city government, from the statistics bureau), of whom 15.11
  million hold Shanghai hukou; GDP ¥5.67 trillion in 2025 (+5.4 %, statistical bulletin of 30 March 2026), the
  largest of any Chinese city; 6,340 km² in 16 districts, including Chongming island in the Yangtze estuary.
- **Story.** A cotton and fishing town walled in 1553 against pirates; opened as a treaty port after the Opium War
  (Treaty of Nanking, 1842). The British and American International Settlement and the French Concession made it
  the "Paris of the East" of banks, jazz, gangsters and refugees (about 20,000 Jews found shelter in Hongkou in the
  1930s–40s). The Chinese Communist Party held its first congress here in 1921. After decades of stagnation, Pudong
  was opened in 1990 and turned farmland into Lujiazui's skyline. The 2010 World Expo and the 2013 Free Trade Zone
  followed. Locals speak Shanghainese 上海话 among themselves; Mandarin works everywhere.
- **Districts** (Puxi 浦西, west of the Huangpu River, is the old city; Pudong 浦东, east, the new):
  - *Huangpu* 黄浦: the Bund 外滩, People's Square 人民广场, Nanjing Road East 南京东路, Yu Garden 豫园, Xintiandi
    新天地.
  - *Xuhui* 徐汇 and *Jing'an* 静安: the former French Concession 法租界 (Wukang Road 武康路, Anfu Road 安福路,
    Fuxing Road; villas, lane houses, cafés), Xujiahui 徐家汇, the West Bund 西岸 galleries, and Nanjing Road West
    南京西路 with its office towers and malls.
  - *Changning* 长宁: Gubei 古北 (Japanese, Korean and Taiwanese families), Zhongshan Park; Hongqiao airport sits on
    its border with *Minhang* 闵行, which holds the Hongqiao transport hub 虹桥枢纽 and international-school villas.
  - *Pudong* 浦东: Lujiazui 陆家嘴 (finance), Century Park 世纪公园, Jinqiao 金桥 (expat villas, factories),
    Zhangjiang 张江 (chips, biotech), Shanghai Disney Resort, Pudong airport, and Lingang 临港 (Tesla Gigafactory).
  - *Hongkou* 虹口 (North Bund, Jewish refugee quarter), *Yangpu* 杨浦 (Fudan and Tongji universities, waterfront),
    *Qingpu* 青浦 (National Exhibition and Convention Center 国家会展中心, Zhujiajiao), *Jiading* 嘉定 (F1, cars).
- **Climate.** Humid subtropical. The **plum rains** 梅雨 (mid-June to early July) bring weeks of drizzle; July–August
  is hot and sticky (35–38 °C, heat alerts above 40); **typhoon season** July–September (Bebinca in September 2024
  was reported as the strongest to hit the city since 1949; flights, ferries and parks shut at orange/red alerts). Winters are
  damp and grey, 0–8 °C, and buildings have **no central heating** (wall air-conditioners heat) — colder indoors
  than Beijing. Spring (April–May) and autumn (October–November) are best. Winter brings occasional haze from the
  north; check an AQI app.

## Entering China and getting in from the airports
- **Visas (changes often; checked October 2026 — confirm with `entry-requirements` or nia.gov.cn).** China's
  unilateral **30-day visa-free entry** for ordinary passports (tourism, business, visiting family, transit), extended
  to 31 December 2026, covers nearly all EU states, Switzerland, Norway, Australia, New Zealand and others; **the UK
  and Canada** were added from 17 February 2026. **Czech and Lithuanian** passports are not on the unilateral list.
  **US citizens are not on it either** and use the **240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit**: 57 nationalities
  (including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and EU states, list of 20 August 2026), 65 designated ports in 24
  provincial-level regions, a confirmed onward ticket to a **third** country or region (Hong Kong and Macau count;
  returning to where you came from does not), and travel allowed across the 24 regions — Shanghai to Suzhou,
  Hangzhou or Beijing is fine. Pudong and Hongqiao airports and the Shanghai cruise port are designated ports.
  Otherwise apply for an L (tourist) or M (business) visa beforehand; there is no visa on arrival.
- **Arrival card.** Since 20 November 2025 foreigners can file it online before travel (free: s.nia.gov.cn, the NIA
  12367 app, or the WeChat/Alipay mini-program), or on paper or a kiosk at the airport. Beware paid look-alike sites.
  See `cn-arrival-card`.
- **Registration.** Hotels register you automatically; in a private home, register at the police station 派出所
  within 24 hours.
- **Pudong International (PVG)** 浦东国际机场, 40 km east, Terminals 1 and 2 (T3 under construction, due 2028); almost
  all long-haul flights land here. To the city:
  - **Maglev** 磁浮 to Longyang Road 龙阳路 (Metro 2/7/16/18) in about 8 minutes, cruising at 300 km/h (the 431 km/h
    runs stopped in 2021); ¥50 one way, ¥40 with a same-day boarding pass, roughly 06:45–21:40 (verify). You still
    need the metro or a taxi from Longyang Road.
  - **Metro Line 2** 地铁2号线 to People's Square in about an hour, about ¥7 — cheapest and slowest.
  - **Taxi** about ¥180 and 50 minutes to People's Square (city government figure); rank outside arrivals (T1 gate
    12, T2 gate 25). Ignore touts.
  - **Airport Link Line** 机场联络线 (opened December 2024) to Hongqiao in about 40 minutes, ¥26 end to end at
    launch, roughly 06:00–22:00, trains about every 15 minutes.
- **Hongqiao (SHA)** 虹桥国际机场, 13 km west: domestic flights plus Tokyo Haneda, Seoul Gimpo, Hong Kong and Taipei
  Songshan; terminal 2 is joined to **Hongqiao railway station** 虹桥火车站 (high-speed trains). Metro Lines 2, 10
  and 17; taxi to the centre about 30 minutes, ¥100–150 (city government figure).

## Money
- Currency is the renminbi (RMB, ¥, CNY). No tipping; hotel restaurants add a service charge.
- **Mobile payment is universal**: Alipay 支付宝 and WeChat Pay 微信支付 accept foreign Visa, Mastercard, Amex, JCB
  and Discover linked in the app; no Chinese bank account needed. Set both up **before arrival** (passport and a
  phone number that receives SMS). Typical terms (October 2026, verify in the app): no fee on payments up to ¥200, 3 %
  above; limits for verified foreign users of about **US$5,000 per payment and US$50,000 a year**; no sending money
  to individuals or red packets.
- Foreign cards are accepted more widely than elsewhere in China (hotels, malls, Bund restaurants, many museums),
  but QR payment is still the default. Cash is legal tender; keep small notes. Bank of China, ICBC and HSBC ATMs
  take foreign cards.
- Expense receipts are **fapiao** 发票: ask at payment.
- **Tax refund**: departure refunds at "Tax Free" shops (Nanjing Road, Lujiazui, Xintiandi malls), many with
  refund on the spot; rules upgraded July 2026 (no paper forms, 28 days to leave from any port). See
  `tax-refund-shopping`.

## Getting online and the apps that matter
- **The Great Firewall** blocks Google (search, Gmail, Maps, Drive), WhatsApp, Signal, Facebook, Instagram, X, YouTube,
  Wikipedia, many Western news sites and most Western AI services. Install and test a **VPN before arrival** (tolerated
  for individuals); **roaming or a travel eSIM** (Airalo, Holafly, a Hong Kong SIM) is *not* filtered — easiest for a
  short trip. A local SIM (China Mobile, Unicom, Telecom; passport required) is filtered.
- **Must-have apps**: WeChat 微信 and Alipay 支付宝 (payments, mini-programs, DiDi, metro QR), Amap 高德地图
  (English mode; Google Maps does not work), DiDi 滴滴 (no Uber), Meituan 美团 and Dianping 大众点评 (Dianping is how
  Shanghai picks restaurants), Trip.com and 12306 for trains, Metro Daduhui 大都会 (official metro QR app),
  Xiaohongshu 小红书 / RedNote (where locals find cafés, exhibitions and shops).
- Hotels: only hotels licensed for foreigners can check you in; filter for "accepts foreigners" on Trip.com.

## Getting around
- **Metro** 地铁: 906 km, 523 stations at end-2025 by the operator's count (including the maglev; the Ministry of
  Transport lists 881.5 km, August 2026), one of the two largest networks in the world alongside Beijing. Roughly
  05:30–23:00, later on some lines at weekends. Fares currently ¥3 for 6 km plus ¥1 per further 10 km; a **fare
  reform** (the first since 2005, average about +¥1, with multi-ride passes) went to public hearing on 7 September
  2026 and was not yet decided in October 2026. Pay with the Alipay/WeChat/Metro Daduhui QR, a Shanghai Public
  Transportation Card 交通卡, or the ticket machines. Security screening at every station. Line 2 crosses the city
  east–west (Hongqiao–Nanjing Road–Lujiazui–Pudong airport), Line 10 Hongqiao–Xintiandi, Line 1 north–south through
  People's Square, Line 17 out to Zhujiajiao.
- **Taxis** start at ¥14 for 3 km (verify); turquoise Dazhong 大众 and white Qiangsheng 强生 are the big fleets; DiDi
  is easier. Drivers rarely speak English — show the address in Chinese.
- **Ferries** across the Huangpu (e.g. Jinling Road 金陵东路 to Lujiazui) cost about ¥2 and give the Bund view for
  pocket change.
- **Rail**: Hongqiao 虹桥 is the main HSR hub (Beijing South about 4.5 h on the fastest trains, Suzhou 25–30 min,
  Hangzhou about 1 h, Nanjing about 1–1.5 h); Shanghai station 上海站 and Shanghai South 上海南 serve older routes;
  **Shanghai East** 上海东站 in Pudong is due to open in July 2027. Book with passport on 12306/Trip.com.
- Shared bikes (Meituan, HelloBike) suit the French Concession and the 45 km Huangpu riverside path.

## The business scene and customs
- **Why it matters.** China's financial centre — the Shanghai Stock Exchange (1990) and its STAR Market 科创板
  (2019) for tech listings, the gold and futures exchanges, Lujiazui banks and asset managers — and the city with
  the most regional headquarters of foreign multinationals in China. Strong in cars (SAIC, Tesla's Gigafactory in
  Lingang, NIO), chips (SMIC, Zhangjiang), biotech, aircraft (COMAC, the C919), steel (Baowu), shipping and trade;
  consumer internet (Trip.com, Bilibili, Xiaohongshu, miHoYo, PDD's operations) and AI start-ups (MiniMax, StepFun).
  Free Trade Zone (2013) and the Lingang special area (2019) offer easier rules for foreign investment.
- **Events**: China International Import Expo, **CIIE, 5–10 November 2026** at the National Exhibition and
  Convention Center (Hongqiao; hotels fill and traffic controls apply), World Artificial Intelligence Conference
  (July), Auto Shanghai (April, odd years — next 2027), Chinese Grand Prix F1 (spring, Jiading), Shanghai Masters
  tennis (October).
- **Business customs**: more international and faster than Beijing — meetings get to the point, English is common in
  multinationals, and contracts and lawyers carry more weight than banquets. Still: exchange **WeChat** first,
  cards with both hands, respect seniority, expect indirect "no"s ("we'll study it"), let the host order and toast,
  reply to WeChat quickly, and use bilingual contracts with the Chinese text prevailing.
- Work permits: Z visa plus work permit (degree plus two years' experience as a rule); the employer handles it.

## Living here
- **Where expats live**: the *former French Concession* (Xuhui/Jing'an: lane houses 弄堂, art-deco flats, cafés —
  walkable, central, older buildings); *Jing'an* around Nanjing Road West (serviced apartments, offices);
  *Gubei/Hongqiao* 古北/虹桥 (families, Japanese and Korean schools, near the airport); *Jinqiao/Biyun* 金桥/碧云 in
  Pudong (villa compounds near Concordia and Dulwich, car or school bus life); *Lujiazui* (towers, finance); *Huacao
  华漕 / Xujing 徐泾* (Minhang/Qingpu villas near SAS Puxi and WISS).
- **Costs** (approximate, 2026): a one-bedroom in Jing'an or the French Concession about ¥8,000–15,000 a month,
  ¥4,500–7,000 further out, villas ¥30,000+; local meal ¥30–60, Western ¥150–350, café latte ¥30–40. One month's
  deposit plus one to three months' rent in advance; agents (Lianjia/Beike 贝壳, Ziroom 自如) charge up to a month.
  Check the heating (an air-conditioner in every room) and dehumidifiers for the plum rains.
- **Health**: Shanghai United Family Hospital 上海和睦家医院 (Changning, plus a Pudong hospital), Jiahui
  International Hospital 嘉会国际医院 (Xuhui), Parkway Health 百汇医疗 clinics, and the international departments of
  top public hospitals — Huashan 华山医院 and Ruijin 瑞金医院. English-speaking, 24-hour emergency at the private
  ones; Western prices, so carry insurance. Emergency 120 is Mandarin only — call your hospital's hotline.
- **Schools** (published fees roughly ¥250,000–390,000 a year for 2026/27; apply early): Shanghai American School
  (Puxi and Pudong), Concordia International School (Jinqiao), Dulwich College Shanghai, Wellington College
  International Shanghai, YCIS Shanghai, British International School Shanghai, Shanghai Community International
  School, Western International School of Shanghai (WISS).
- **Daily life**: very safe day and night; the main scams are "tea house" and "art student" invitations around
  Nanjing Road and People's Square. Rubbish must be sorted (four bins, enforced since 2019). Smoking is banned indoors.

## Food
- **Shanghainese** 本帮菜: xiaolongbao 小笼包 (Jia Jia Tang Bao 佳家汤包), pan-fried shengjian 生煎 (Yang's 小杨生煎),
  red-braised pork 红烧肉, scallion-oil noodles 葱油拌面, drunken chicken, smoked fish, and **hairy crab** 大闸蟹
  (October–December). Sweeter and oilier than northern food.
- Where: Huanghe Road 黄河路 (old-school restaurants, famous from the TV series *Blossoms Shanghai* 繁花), Yunnan
  Road for snacks, the French Concession for cafés, bakeries and international food, Bund rooftops for cocktails
  with the Pudong view. The Michelin Guide's first mainland edition was Shanghai (2016). Vegetarian: 我吃素 (Gongdelin
  功德林); halal 清真.

## What to see (a weekend, plus day trips)
- **Book ahead, with your passport**: the Shanghai Museum (People's Square) and Shanghai Museum East 上海博物馆东馆
  (Pudong) are free with timed reservations; Shanghai Tower and Disney sell timed tickets online.
- **Day 1**: the Bund 外滩 in the morning → Nanjing Road East → Yu Garden 豫园 and the old town → Shanghai Museum →
  Xintiandi and the First Party Congress site 中共一大会址 → back to the Bund after dark for the lit Pudong skyline.
- **Day 2**: French Concession walk (Wukang Road, Wukang Mansion 武康大楼, Anfu Road, Tianzifang 田子坊) → Jing'an
  Temple 静安寺 → ferry to Pudong → Shanghai Tower 上海中心 (632 m, the tallest in China; observation deck on 118) or
  the West Bund galleries (Long Museum 龙美术馆) at sunset. Also: Jade Buddha Temple 玉佛寺, Jewish Refugees Museum
  上海犹太难民纪念馆 (Hongkou), Shanghai Disney Resort 上海迪士尼度假区.
- **Day trips**: Suzhou 苏州 (classical gardens, canals; 25–30 min by HSR), Hangzhou 杭州 (West Lake, Longjing tea
  hills; about 1 h), Zhujiajiao 朱家角 water town (Metro Line 17), Wuzhen 乌镇 (about 2 h, best overnight),
  Nanjing 南京 (Ming walls, Sun Yat-sen mausoleum), Moganshan 莫干山 (hills, boutique hotels).
- Avoid Golden Week (1–7 October) and Spring Festival for sights: the Bund gets crowd controls.
